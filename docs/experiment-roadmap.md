# Experiment roadmap

The roadmap is intentionally a decision tool, not an idea backlog. Scores use a 1–5 scale: impact and confidence go up; effort and risk go down. Priority score = `(impact × confidence) / (effort + risk)`.

| Rank | Journey opportunity | Hypothesis | Impact | Confidence | Effort | Risk | Score | Status |
| ---: | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Mobile cover selection | A transparent recommendation reduces comparison effort | 5 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 6.67 | Evaluated |
| 2 | Quote-detail errors | Inline examples for property type reduce correction loops | 4 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 5.33 | Discovery |
| 3 | Price explanation | Monthly and annual framing improves comprehension | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3.00 | Needs research |
| 4 | Returning quote visitors | A saved-quote reminder reduces restart friction | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2.40 | Planned |
| 5 | Add-on selection | Group add-ons by customer goal rather than policy language | 3 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 1.50 | Backlog |

## Cadence across business units

1. Monthly intake: each web team brings one evidenced opportunity.
2. Weekly design review: agree hypothesis, decision owner, metrics, and feasibility.
3. Shared release gate: analytics, legal/compliance, accessibility, and platform owners validate implementation.
4. Fortnightly readout: publish the result, not just wins, in a searchable learning library.

This structure gives seven web teams a common experiment language while retaining local ownership of customer journeys.
